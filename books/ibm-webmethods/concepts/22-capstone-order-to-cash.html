Capstone: an order-to-cash platform

Everything at once. Orders in through a public API, validated and canonicalized, created in the ERP, published as events, consumed by the CRM and fulfilment, reconciled against a supplier's EDI acknowledgement — with the monitoring, retries, tests and deployment that make it supportable.

The whole system

Decompose before you build

The instinct is one long flow service that does all of it. Resist it for a concrete reason: a single service can only be retried as a whole, so one CRM hiccup fails an order that was already in the ERP. Split by failure domain — each piece is a unit you would want to retry independently.

1 — Intake

The gateway carries authentication, threat protection and a Traffic Optimization policy limiting invocations per interval via a token bucket — sized to admit the 09:00 burst of fifty orders while holding the long-run average. Behind it, submitOrder is chapter 5's flow: correlate, validate against CanonicalOrder, check the idempotency key, apply business rules, then the side effect.

2 — Canonical transform and the ERP

Map to CanonicalOrder at the edge, never deeper, so the ERP's field names stop at the mapping boundary. Pass customerPoNumber to the ERP as its own external reference: a repeated create then returns the existing sales order instead of making a second, which is what makes the whole retry story safe. The connection is sized to the ERP's limit divided across nodes, not replicated to them.

3 — Events, published after the commit

One publishable document type, OrderStatusChanged, published after the ERP commit — or via a transactional outbox if the two cannot be made atomic. Three subscribers, none known to the publisher. The CRM notifier batches to stay inside 100 calls a minute; fulfilment reacts per event; analytics consumes the stream. Guaranteed delivery is at-least-once, so all three are idempotent.

4 — The acknowledgement round trip

The supplier drops an EDI 855 on the ActiveTransfer landing area using the agreed completeness convention. Trading Networks recognizes the document type, resolves sender and receiver to partner profiles, selects a processing rule by criteria, and the rule's action invokes the acknowledgement service — asynchronously, since nobody is waiting. The service persists the interchange control number and treats a repeat as a logged no-op.

5 — Reconciliation

The step most capstones omit and every production system needs. A scheduled job compares acknowledged lines against open sales order lines and reports three categories: acknowledged but not ordered, ordered but not acknowledged past the agreed window, and quantity mismatches. Route the report to a human, not to a log. This is also your migration safety net — chapter 20's parallel run uses this same job.

The operational envelope

  • Monitoring. Orders accepted per minute, p99 intake latency, ERP pool utilization, dead-letter depth, event lag per subscriber, and hours since the last supplier file.
  • Alerts. Dead-letter depth above zero; pool utilization sustained above 80%; supplier file absent past its window; reconciliation exceptions above a threshold. Two of the four are absence alarms.
  • Retries. Transient class only, three attempts, exponential with jitter, dead-letter after.
  • Tests. Unit tests on every mapping with the five cases; contract tests on the API and on OrderStatusChanged; integration tests with a mocked ERP that can be slow, refused, half-successful and duplicating; a scheduled partner round-trip suite outside the CI gate.
  • Deployment. One artifact per commit, promoted unchanged; configuration injected; document type changes additive only.

The capstone is not finished when an order flows through. It is finished when you can tell where any order is, and prove which commit is running.

Failure scenario

The team builds all five units and skips reconciliation, because in testing the acknowledgements always matched. In production the supplier acknowledges 98.4% of lines; the missing 1.6% are back-ordered items their system silently omits rather than acknowledging with a zero quantity. Nothing errors anywhere — there is no failed transaction, only an absence — and the gap surfaces as unexplained inventory variance a quarter later. Absence is invisible to every error-driven control in the system, and reconciliation is the only one that looks for it.

Design review questions

  1. For each of the five units: what is its retry unit, and what makes a retry safe?
  2. Is anything published before the transaction that makes it true has committed?
  3. Multiply the ERP pool by your node count. Does it exceed the ERP's limit?
  4. Which of your alerts fire on absence rather than on error?
  5. If the supplier re-sent last week's file tonight, what would happen?
  6. Can you answer "where is PO 4471195" from production tooling in under two minutes?

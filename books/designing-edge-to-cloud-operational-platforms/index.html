Telemetry, streaming, resilience, vision, and optimization for critical industrial systems.

The cloud can optimise operations. The field must remain useful when the cloud, the network, or any individual service is unavailable. Everything in this book follows from taking that sentence literally.

Twenty-one chapters, seven parts Running domain: a connected rail network For engineers moving into architecture

How to read it

Parts I–II establish that the field, not the cloud, is the system's centre of gravity. Part III is the streaming backbone. Part IV makes it degrade safely. Parts V–VI add the two workloads that break the telemetry mould — vision and optimization — and Part VII closes with a capstone and an interview playbook. Every chapter forces the same five choices: partitioning, ordering, failure recovery, offline behaviour, and where the safety boundary sits.

One boundary, stated once and kept

This book teaches high availability and graceful degradation, which are architectural properties you can design and measure. Operational safety in a rail-like domain is bounded explicitly and owned by dedicated systems and procedures under certification regimes a general cloud platform does not participate in. Every chapter says where that line falls rather than blurring it.

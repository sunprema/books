Chapter five

Writing With Receipts

By the time ScientistOne reaches its third stage, it's holding two things: a grounded brief from the Problem Investigator (chapter 3) and a scored, constraint-respecting solution from the Discovery Engine (chapter 4). Writing the paper is where those two evidence trails get turned into prose — and it's also the last place a claim can slip through without a trace, which is exactly why this stage has its own dedicated gatekeeper.

The Claim Verifier's job

The paper describes a component called the Claim Verifier that checks every claim in the draft against its declared evidence source. Read that description carefully: "every claim," not "every claim flagged as risky," and "declared evidence source," meaning the check isn't going out to find new support for a sentence after the fact — it's confirming that the support the sentence was supposedly built from actually holds up.

The order of operations, restated

This is the "by construction" principle from chapter 2 showing up as an actual last gate. A claim enters the draft already carrying a declared source — a citation from the Problem Investigator's reading, a number from a Discovery Engine run, a method description tied to a specific line of code. The Claim Verifier's job isn't to go looking for evidence; it's to confirm the evidence that's already supposed to be there actually is.

Three kinds of sentence, three kinds of check

Because the three claim types from chapter 2 have different trace targets, the Claim Verifier's work looks different depending on what kind of sentence it's checking:

Writing a related-work sentence ✓ citation claim

Before the sentence ships, the Claim Verifier confirms the cited work is in the Problem Investigator's read set and that the sentence's characterization of it matches what that source actually says.

a real, already-read source — not a plausible author-year pattern

Writing a results-table number ✓ numerical claim

Before the number ships, the Claim Verifier confirms it matches the recorded output of the specific Discovery Engine run being reported.

the exploited branch's run log, exactly — not a rounded or "close enough" figure

Writing a method-section sentence ✓ methodological claim

Before the sentence ships, the Claim Verifier confirms the described technique resolves to the corresponding implementation in the submitted code — not a simplified or aspirational version of it.

the actual code that ran, not the code the paper wishes had run

Every one of these checks is mechanical, in the same sense the trace targets in chapter 2 were mechanical. Nothing here requires the Claim Verifier to have deep domain judgment about whether the research is good — only whether each sentence points at something real. That's a deliberately narrow job, and narrow is what makes it tractable to run on every single sentence rather than a sample.

What the discipline buys, in one number

We'll spend chapter 7 on the full results table, but one figure is worth previewing here because it's the direct payoff of this exact stage: across ScientistOne's evaluated papers, references were checked against real sources 337 times, and zero turned out to be hallucinated. Not "rare" — zero. That's what a by-construction Claim Verifier checking every citation before it ships looks like when it's working.

A sentence that can't produce its receipt doesn't get to keep the sentence. It gets rewritten until it can, or it doesn't ship at all.

Contrast that with chapter 1's baseline numbers — hallucinated reference rates up to 21% among systems that write first and, at best, check later. The gap between 21% and 0% isn't a difference in how careful the writing prose is. It's the difference between a system that can produce an unsupported sentence and one that structurally cannot.

So what did we actually gain?

A concrete picture of the last gate in the pipeline: a Claim Verifier that checks every sentence's declared evidence before it's allowed to ship, closing the loop that started with the Problem Investigator's reading and the Discovery Engine's runs. Chapter 6 turns the same kind of checking outward — onto any system's paper, ScientistOne's included, from the outside.

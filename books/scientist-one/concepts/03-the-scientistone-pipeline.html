Chapter three

Three Stages, One Evidence Trail

ScientistOne is described in the paper as an end-to-end autonomous research system that maintains evidence chains by construction throughout three stages: literature review, solution discovery, and paper writing. We spent chapter 2 on why "by construction" matters. This chapter is about the shape of the pipeline itself — three stages that look, on their surface, like ordinary research work, each wired so the evidence trail from the stage before it never breaks.

Stage one: the Problem Investigator

Every research paper starts with reading other research papers, and that step is exactly where a lot of the fabricated-citation problem from chapter 1 gets born — a system that hasn't actually read the literature it's about to cite has nothing to ground those citations in except plausible-sounding pattern completion.

ScientistOne's literature stage is handled by a component the paper calls the Problem Investigator. Its job: read up to 100 full-text PDFs per research topic and turn that reading into a grounded experiment brief — a plan for what to try, anchored in what the literature actually says rather than in a model's compressed memory of what papers in this area tend to say.

Why "full-text," not abstracts

An abstract tells you a paper's headline claim. It doesn't tell you which figure that claim came from, what the actual experimental setup was, or whether a caveat three paragraphs into the discussion section quietly limits how far the claim generalizes. If the Problem Investigator's brief is going to ground later citation claims, it needs the kind of detail only the full text carries — the same reason a human researcher doesn't stop at the abstract either.

This is also where the "by construction" promise starts paying rent. Every source the Problem Investigator actually reads becomes a candidate grounding source for a future citation claim — it's already in the evidence record before the writing stage ever needs it, which is the opposite of a system that writes a citation first and hopes a lookup later confirms it.

The experiment brief as a handoff, not a suggestion

The brief the Problem Investigator produces isn't loose inspiration for the next stage to riff on — it's the grounded starting point that constrains what the Discovery Engine is allowed to explore. That matters for the same reason it matters in any pipeline with a strict contract between stages: if stage two could quietly wander away from what stage one actually found in the literature, the evidence chain built in stage one would be decorative rather than load-bearing.

We're deliberately previewing stages two and three at a summary level here — each gets its own chapter, because each has a distinct verification story worth slowing down for. The Discovery Engine's explore-exploit search is chapter 4; the Claim Verifier's final pass over the draft is chapter 5.

The evidence record isn't a log kept alongside the work. It's the thing each stage hands to the next one instead of a summary.

Why a rigid pipeline, not a looser agent loop

Many autonomous-agent systems favor a looser architecture — a single loop that reads, plans, experiments, and writes somewhat interchangeably, trusting the model's own judgment to sequence the work sensibly. ScientistOne's three-stage structure is a more opinionated choice, and the opinion is specifically about evidence: a fixed stage boundary is also a fixed point where the evidence record must be complete before the next stage is allowed to start consuming it. A looser loop that lets writing begin before discovery has actually finished would reopen exactly the crack CoE is built to close — a claim written before its grounding source exists.

So what did we actually gain?

A concrete picture of where the evidence chain in ScientistOne actually starts — a Problem Investigator reading up to 100 full-text PDFs per topic — and why the pipeline is staged rather than looped: each stage boundary is a place the evidence record has to be complete before the next stage may use it.

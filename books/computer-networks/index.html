How this book talks

You already know what a packet is. This book is about the harder part: why the network was shaped this way, what the boxes inside it are really doing, and what goes wrong when it gets big. It says all of that in plain words. When a real technical word can't be avoided, it shows up once, marked like this, with a plain meaning right beside it — and then the plain words take over again.

Every sheet opens with a small scene — a kitchen, a post office, a crowded road — where something goes wrong. Then it shows the fix inside that same scene, says honestly what the fix costs, and ends with one sentence about why it's worth it. Along the way you'll meet these drawings:

  • Two pans of a scale — a choice where getting more of one good thing means giving up another.
  • Bit rulers — the exact shape of a message, 32 little boxes to a row.
  • Worked numbers — the sums that tell you how big, how slow, or how full.
  • Real cases — what happened when real networks met real trouble.
  • Check yourself — something to work out, with the answer hidden until you ask.

The One-Page Cheat Card

The real name: “Cheatsheet: products, risk words, and the whole book in 30 seconds”
Keep this one

Tear this out, pin it up, print it. First the whole book in half a minute. Then one card for each kind of paper, then a small dictionary of the risk words. Numbers in brackets point to the chapter that explains them.

The book in 30 seconds

  1. A bond is a pile of promised payments. Its price is those promises, each shrunk for the wait, added up. Higher yield, lower price; lower yield, higher price. (1, 11)
  2. Quote clean, pay dirty. Dirty = clean + accrued interest. Trades settle one business day after the trade (T+1) for US company bonds. (1)
  3. Every bond is a bet on something: rates (Treasuries), rates plus credit (company bonds), prepayment (mortgage pools), waterfall luck (ABS), and so on. (2)
  4. A position is what the mandate allows. One trade has several truths: front office, settlement, custody, books. Reconcile them. (3)
  5. Trading is mostly asking dealers. Ask several (RFQ), watch axes, save every quote and the reason. (4)
  6. Check the other side and the limits before the trade, not after. Exposure goes down with netting, collateral and central clearing. (5)
  7. Price against curves, not single points. Spread tells you what you are paid for credit, liquidity and options. (6)
  8. Risk is measured in dollars per basis point: DV01 for rates, CS01 for credit, key-rate DV01 for curve shape, convexity for big moves. (7)
  9. Nothing is free to hold. Repo funds it, a haircut is your own cash in it, margin moves with prices, and fails cost money. (8)
  10. Know which price you are looking at: executed, quoted, or modeled, and who checked it. (9)
  11. Explain tomorrow’s change dollar by dollar: carry + roll + rates + spread + convexity + hedge slippage + residual. (7, 10)

One question per chapter: what is held, why, how it was traded, how it is paid for, how it is valued, what can lose money, and who is keeping it inside the lines.

Part B. One card for each kind of paper

Treasuries (government paper) (2)

PiecePlain facts
Bills4 weeks to 1 year. Sold at a discount, no coupon.
Notes / bondsNotes run 2–10 years, bonds 20–30. Coupons twice a year.
TIPSThe principal moves with consumer prices (CPI).
On-the-runNewest auctioned issue of each length; the benchmark. About two-thirds of Treasury trading volume.
Off-the-runOlder issues; trade far less.
Main riskRates. Day count is actual/actual (11).

Company bonds (corporates) (1, 2)

PiecePlain facts
Investment grade / high yieldIG is BBB-/Baa3 or higher; HY is below that.
Pecking orderSenior secured, senior unsecured, subordinated. Losses hit the bottom first.
Early-exit tricksCallable: issuer may repay early. Putable: you may demand early repayment. Convertible: swap for shares.
Floating-rate notesCoupon resets to a reference rate plus a fixed spread.
Day countUsually 30/360. US trades settle T+1.
Main risksCredit spread, default, rates, thin trading.

City and state bonds (munis) (2)

PiecePlain facts
General obligation (GO)Backed by full faith, credit and taxing power.
RevenueBacked only by one project’s income.
TaxInterest on most is free of federal income tax, so issuers borrow more cheaply.
CallsMost can be called, commonly after about ten years.
Main risksProject or issuer credit, call risk.

Bundled loans (securitized) (2, 6)

PiecePlain facts
Agency MBSMortgage pools with a timely-payment guarantee from Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac or Ginnie Mae. Main risk is early payback, not credit.
ContractionRates fall, owners refinance, money returns early at the worst time.
ExtensionRates rise, payback slows, the bond gets longer.
Negative convexityRises less than a plain bond when rates fall; can fall more when they rise.
ABSCar, card, student loans. Payments run down a waterfall: senior first, losses hit the junior slice first.
CushionsSubordination, overcollateralization, excess spread.

Contracts built on paper (derivatives) (2)

PiecePlain facts
Interest-rate swapFixed payments traded for floating (now SOFR-based) on a notional that is not itself swapped.
SwaptionAn option to enter a swap.
Treasury futuresThe seller may deliver any bond in a basket. The cheapest-to-deliver bond sets how the future behaves.
CDSPremium leg: buyer pays a running spread (act/360). Protection leg: on a credit event the seller pays par minus recovery. Fair value = both legs equal.
Credit eventsBankruptcy, failure to pay, restructuring.
Contract setISDA Master, Schedule, Confirmations, Credit Support Annex; netting on default. (5)

Borrowing products (funding) (2, 8)

PiecePlain facts
RepoSell with a promise to buy back higher. A loan against collateral; the price gap is the interest.
Reverse repoThe lender’s side: cash out, securities in.
FlavorsOvernight, term, open (rolls daily). Tri-party hands collateral choice, valuation and custody to a clearing bank.
GC vs specialGC takes any fitting security. A bond in demand trades special, at a lower cash rate.
Haircut$98 cash against $100 of bonds is a 2% haircut.
MarginVariation margin covers today’s mark; initial margin covers possible future loss.
ContractGMRA for repo. (5)
SettlementDelivery versus payment (DVP). A fail costs money (TMPG charge up to 3% a year minus the reference rate).

Part C. The risk words, plainly

WordPlain meaningHandy form
DurationPercent of price lost for a 1% rise in yield. Straight-line guess; small moves only. Macaulay = average wait; modified = Macaulay ÷ (1 + y/n); effective = found by re-pricing up and down, needed for callables and MBS.3y 5% bond: 2.82 / 2.75
DV01 / PV01Dollars lost per 0.01% rise in yield. The unit for adding up and hedging rate risk.mod dur × MV ÷ 10,000
Key-rate DV01DV01 split by curve point (2y, 5y, 10y, 30y). Zero total can still lose on a steepening.sums to DV01
ConvexityThe bend duration misses. Positive: gain more, lose less on big moves. Callables and MBS bend the wrong way.−D·Δy + ½·C·Δy²
CS01Dollars for a 1bp move in credit spread. Companions: spread duration, credit beta, jump-to-default, recovery, migration.per 1bp of spread
CarryWhat holding earns: coupon accrual plus pull to par, less funding if borrowed.income − funding
Roll (roll-down)Price gain from sliding to a shorter point on an upward-sloping curve that stays put. Carry + roll = expected return if nothing moves.curve held still
Z-spreadThe one spread added to every point of the zero curve so the discounted payments equal the dirty price.example: ~104bp
OASZ-spread with the option’s value taken out, using a volatility model. Callable: OAS below Z. Putable: above.Z − option cost
BasisA gap between two prices for nearly the same risk (cash–futures, CDS–bond, swap spread, cross-currency). It lasts because of funding, collateral, liquidity, delivery options and balance-sheet room. CDS–bond basis = CDS spread − bond spread.price A − price B
BetaHow much one position moves per move in a reference (for credit: credit beta, against a spread index). It is a fitted number, so check what it was fitted to.move ÷ reference move
HaircutThe cushion between collateral value and cash lent, sized by collateral quality and the borrower. Bigger in stress.1 − cash ÷ collateral

Sums to keep in your pocket (11)

Present value = payment ÷ (1 + r)^t  ·  $100 in 3 years at 5% = $86.38

Dirty = clean + accrued  ·  accrued = coupon per period × days accrued ÷ days in period

Rates P&L ≈ − DV01 × move in bp  ·  $80,000 × 6bp ≈ − $480,000

Hedge size = bond DV01 ÷ hedge DV01 per unit

Total P&L = carry/roll + rates + spread + convexity + hedge slippage + residual

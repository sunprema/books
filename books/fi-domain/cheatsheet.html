Everything You Need on One Desk, Short and Plain

Real name: “Cheatsheet”

Print this. Pin it up. Each part below holds the small facts of one chapter: the rules, the numbers, and the mistakes that bite. For the reasons behind them, go back to the chapter. Rules that change over time (contract sizes, venue details, stress paths) are marked check live. Read them from the source on the day you need them.

The 30-second summary, chapter by chapter

#In thirty seconds
01Every payment needs a date and a count of days. Pick the day count and calendar first, then draw the line of values. Change the drawing rule and the in-between numbers change.
02One number says how much you lose if rates move one small step. Count it the same way everywhere. For big moves, the one number is not enough.
03Three things to hold: government loans, date-promises on them (futures), and swapped payments. Futures follow one cheapest bond, and that bond can change.
04Holding pays you (coupon) and costs you (funding). Waiting moves you down the line. Together they are a baseline, not a forecast.
05The quoted gap is the smallest cost. Big size costs more. Futures come in whole contracts only.
06Name the surprise, then name the curve move it makes. Build made-up scenarios from level, slope and curve, so the tenors stay in step.
07Zero risk on paper is not zero risk. Match each length, keep to the desk rules, solve, round, then check again.
08Right inputs, right time, right ID. Flag bad data. Never fill a hole with a zero or with yesterday and say nothing.
09List it, have someone else check it, keep it inside fences, write it all down. The engine counts. The helper talks.

01 · Curves and conventions

WhatRule
Treasury notes and bondsActual/Actual (ICMA): accrued share = actual days since last coupon ÷ (days in coupon period × coupons a year). A 182-day half-year gives 364.
SOFR swap, both legsAnnual payments, ACT/360, fixed leg too. Cleared venues allow other fixed day counts, so confirm per trade.
30/360 fixed legThe older, LIBOR-era convention. The brief lists it; the SOFR market uses ACT/360 annual. Know which one your trade uses.
Dirty priceClean price + accrued interest. Treasuries trade on clean price.
SettlementTreasuries regular-way settle next business day (T+1), which is market practice. A Friday trade changes the accrued days.
Log-linear on discount factorsSame as flat forwards between nodes, so forwards jump at each node.
Monotone convex (Hagan & West)Smooth forwards that stay positive when the input forwards are positive.
Treasury curveThe daily par yield curve is built from the newest issues at fixed tenors. A fitted spline uses all bonds.
Discounting curveSOFR OIS: the overnight rate, compounded daily. The standard USD choice for cleared swaps.
 
q      = ql.SimpleQuote(0.0425)
h      = ql.QuoteHandle(q)
helper = ql.OISRateHelper(2, ql.Period('5Y'), h, sofr_index)
curve  = ql.PiecewiseLogLinearDiscount(today, [helper], ql.Actual365Fixed())
handle = ql.RelinkableYieldTermStructureHandle(curve)
q.setValue(0.0430)   # curve notices; rebuilds on next query
GotchasACT/360 against ACT/ACT moves a rate by about 1.4% of itself, which is silent wrong carry. Coupon periods run 181 to 184 days, so accrued interest differs. A relinked handle changes every thing that hangs on it: good for forks, bad if shared by accident.

02 · Risk measures

WhatRule
DV01Modified duration × price × 0.0001. Here: the loss for +1bp, positive for a long bond. State your sign.
Portfolio DV01Sum of position DV01s, if all use the same parallel shift of the same curve. It hides offsets at different tenors.
Key-rate DV01Triangle bump peaking at a key tenor, fading to its neighbors. Central difference: (P− − P+) ÷ 2. Partials sum to the parallel number.
Bump par vs bump zeroPar: risk per instrument you can hedge. Zero: risk per curve node. State which.
Bump sizeSmall enough to stay local, big enough to avoid noise. 1bp central is a custom, not a law.
Taylor (duration + convexity)Fine for small moves. Sources say add convexity beyond about 25–50bp; reprice fully for big moves, curve-shaped moves and options.
Curve wordsBull = yields down, bear = yields up. Steepener/flattener = long minus short widens/narrows. Butterfly = belly against wings.
P&L splitCarry, roll-down, curve move, spread change, residual.
10y, 4% semiannual bond at 4% yield, price 100: shockFull repriceDV01 onlyDV01 + convexity
+25bp−2.0195−2.0439−2.0193
+100bp−7.7946−8.1757−7.7812
GotchasMixed sign rules flip hedges. Do not add DV01 across curves with no shared curve. One-sided bumps lean on convexity. A residual in the P&L split is a bug alarm, not noise to hide.

03 · Instruments

ThingFact
Bills / notes / bondsBills: a year or less, sold at a discount. Notes: 2 to 10 years. Bonds: 20 and 30 years.
AuctionSingle price: all winners get the highest accepted yield. No-risk bids fill first.
STRIPSZero-coupon pieces cut from a note or bond. A 10y note makes 21.
Newest vs older issueThe newest of a maturity is richer and easier to trade. The gap is a liquidity premium that grows in stress.
Tick sizes10y and Ultra 10y: ½ of 1/32 = $15.625. Bond and Ultra Bond: 1/32 = $31.25. 5y: ¼ of 1/32 = $7.8125. 2y: ⅛ of 1/32 = $15.625. Check live.
Contract cycleMar/Jun/Sep/Dec, physical delivery. Face $100k, except 2y at $200k.
Conversion factorRestates a bond as if it yielded 6%. Invoice = futures settle × CF + accrued.
Gross basisCash price − futures price × CF.
Net basisGross basis − carry. Lowest net basis = highest implied repo = cheapest to deliver.
Futures DV01≈ CTD DV01 ÷ CF. Example: 0.0700 ÷ 0.8000 = 0.0875 per 100; one $100k contract = $87.50 per bp.
Short’s optionsWhich bond, which day, end of month, and the wild card (futures settle 2pm Chicago; the short may call delivery later that evening).
SOFR OIS swapAnnual both legs, ACT/360, New York calendar, 2-business-day payment lag, daily compounded floating leg.
Cleared swap marginThe central clearing house sets it. Close-out horizon up to five days for swaps against one for futures, so swap margin runs higher.
Swap spreadSwap rate − Treasury yield, same maturity. Long ends have been negative for years. Drivers: dealer balance-sheet limits, pension-style receive-fixed demand, Treasury supply, the LIBOR exit.
GotchasDividing by CF is right only while that bond is the CTD, so recompute after a switch. Highest implied repo, lowest net basis and CTD all point to one bond. A swap spread is mostly funding, balance sheet and supply, not credit. Do not use stale contract specs.

04 · Carry, roll and funding

WhatRule
CarryCoupon accrual − financing cost over the horizon. Can be positive or negative.
Roll-downPrice gain from aging along an unchanged upward curve. Largest where the curve is steepest.
Total returnCarry + roll-down + price change from curve moves.
BreakevenAbout (carry + roll) ÷ DV01: the rise in yield that wipes out the gain. State your funding and reinvestment rules.
GC vs special repoGC is driven by funding cost; special by demand for one bond. Specials trade below GC, so owning one lowers your financing and lifts carry.
Fails chargeMax(3% − reference rate, 0) a year. It caps the fail part of specialness only when rates are under 3%. Check live.
Implied repoThe return from buy cash, sell future, deliver. It is not a quoted repo rate. Beats your repo rate = looks good before costs and options.
Swap carryFixed rate minus the forward floating rate over the period, plus roll-down on the remaining life.
Time-roll shockMove the valuation date forward, hold the curve (or its forwards) fixed, reprice. The difference less accrual is roll-down. Say which curve rule you held.

Worked case: 5y 4% note at par, 1 month, financing 3.60%. Carry 0.3333 − 0.3000 = 0.0333. Roll-down 0.0710. Total 0.1044 per 100. The curve slope here is made up.

GotchasAccrual is carry, not roll. Do not use GC for a bond that trades special. Say which day count an annual carry uses. A hedge’s carry drag is a cost: say it per month, in dollars.

05 · Liquidity and transaction costs

WhatRule
Quoted spreadThe first liquidity sign. A round trip pays all of it; one way about half, before any size cost.
Newest issuesUnder 4% of Treasury debt but about 65% of daily volume (TRACE, Jun 2026, mostly 2-year). With age, volume falls and spreads widen, except for bonds cheapest to deliver.
StressQuoted spreads can stay tight while depth and volume fall. Look at depth too.
Square-root ruleImpact of a big order Q grows about with volatility × √(Q ÷ daily volume). Exponent usually 0.4–0.7. Mostly measured on stocks and futures, so calibrate on your own data.
TRACETreasury trades are reported to FINRA and made public next day. Rules may change.
VenuesDealer-to-client: mostly request-for-quote (Tradeweb, Bloomberg). Between dealers: mostly order book (BrokerTec, with a Chicago book from Oct 2025). Futures: CME.
Swap costA spread in bp on the rate, turned into dollars by the trade’s DV01.
Block minimumsCheck live. Sources for the bond disagree (2,000 against 3,000).
 
cost_usd    = half_spread_bp * dv01_usd + impact_bp * dv01_usd   # bp x $/bp
n_contracts = round(target_dv01 / fut_dv01)                      # whole contracts only
GotchasThe quoted gap is not the whole cost for big size. One spread for all tenors and ages is wrong. A score nobody can explain is no use: show every raw input. After rounding, check the DV01 left over.

06 · Scenarios and the macro context

SurpriseUsual curve moveWhy
Hawkish (higher expected path)Bear flattenerShort yields rise more than long.
Dovish (lower expected path)Bull steepenerShort yields fall more than long.
Supply or term-premium fearBear steepenerLong end rises more.
Growth scareBull flattenerLong end rallies more.
WhatFact
FOMCEight meetings a year. Statement 2:00pm ET on day two, press talk 2:30pm. Dot plot only in March, June, September, December. The 2026 dates run Jan 27–28 to Dec 8–9. The dots are single views, not a promise.
Data and supplyCPI and payrolls at 8:30am ET. Refunding news in early Feb/May/Aug/Nov, then 3-, 10- and 30-year auctions.
2013 taper tantrum10-year from about 1.6–1.7% in early May to about 3.0% by early September. Starting levels differ by source. It was not shown to be a clean steepener.
2022 hikesFunds rate 0–0.25% (Mar 2022) to 5.25–5.50% (Jul 2023), 525bp. Bear flattening; 2s10s inverted about −100bp, deepest since 1981.
PCALevel, slope, curvature explain nearly all the variance (about 99%+ for three). Shock the factors, map back to tenors by loadings.
CCAR / DFASTThe Fed publishes a baseline and a severely adverse path each year. The 2026 severely adverse path takes the 3-month yield from 3.7% to 0.1% in two quarters. Check live; it is new each year.
GotchasNot every rise in yields is hawkish flattening. Tenors shocked one by one give kinks and odd forwards. Quote history with its date window. A stress path is a severity story, not a forecast.

07 · Hedging and optimization

WhatRule
DV01-neutralZero change for a 1bp parallel move. Leaves slope, curve, convexity and basis risk.
Key-rate matchedDV01 near zero at each key tenor, using several instruments.
Futures countPosition DV01 ÷ futures DV01 (≈ CTD DV01 ÷ CF). Re-run if the CTD switches.
Futures against cashBasis risk from CTD, delivery options, financing.
Swap against TreasuryBasis risk is the swap spread.
Long-end toolsUltra Bond futures, 20y/30y SOFR swaps, cash bonds. They differ in basis, ease of trade and margin. Verify exchange codes live.
Mandate boundsCash often cannot go short (lower bound 0). Futures and swaps take either sign. Desks cap size, DV01 and gross notional.
Problem classSquared residual + L1 cost + linear bounds = convex QP. L1 residual alone = LP. L1 cost gives few trades. Whole contracts make it non-convex.
Solverscvxpy ships Clarabel, OSQP and SCS. Force one with prob.solve(solver=cp.CLARABEL).
After the solveRead prob.status: optimal, infeasible, unbounded, or an _inaccurate kind. Report infeasible; do not guess.
 
import cvxpy as cp
w     = cp.Variable(n)                 # units of each hedge
resid = A @ w + book                   # key-rate DV01 left over
obj   = cp.sum_squares(resid) + lam * (c @ cp.abs(w))
prob  = cp.Problem(cp.Minimize(obj), [w >= lo, w <= hi])
prob.solve()
GotchasA DV01-neutral book can still lose on a steepener. A wrong or switched CTD mis-sizes the hedge. Swap hedges on Treasuries add swap-spread risk. Unscaled variables make solvers fail. Rounding can break limits, so re-check key rates. Never relax a limit without saying so.

08 · Data and operations

WhatRule
CUSIP / ISINCUSIP: 9 characters, the last a check digit. ISIN: 12 characters (country, 9-character local ID, check digit); US ISINs wrap the CUSIP.
Reopening vs new issueA reopening keeps the CUSIP and is fungible. A new issue gets a new one.
Trade-date vs settle-dateTrade-date includes unsettled trades; settle-date only settled ones. Treasuries settle T+1.
Treasury par curveBuilt from bid-side quotes for the newest issues, collected by the New York Fed about 3:30pm each business day.
SOFRPublished by the New York Fed about 8:00am ET, for the day before.
FRED DGS1010-year constant maturity, from the H.15 release. It has gaps on non-business days.
BVALEvaluated prices from snapshots through the day, each with a score. Read exact times from your data license.
Failed checkFrame it with SR 11-7: validate inputs, control changes, and write down what happened.
GotchasA missing holiday value is not zero. Do not mix 3:30pm quotes with a mark from another time in one curve. Do not carry yesterday’s mark without a stale flag. Odd negative forwards: flag, do not auto-fix. Match by CUSIP, never by name or ticker.

09 · Governance and model risk

WhatRule
What counts as a model (SR 11-7)A method that turns input data into numbers, with input, processing and reporting parts. Judgment-heavy methods count too. Your engine counts.
Three keysBuild and use it well; independent validation with effective challenge; governance with policies and a list of models.
Ongoing watchCompare outcomes to results and to a second method. Thresholds and escalation steps come from practice, not the letter.
Matching the bank’s systemSame positions date basis, marks, curves, conventions, bump size. Explain each gap by bucket.
Independent price checkA unit apart from the dealing room, at least monthly (Basel-derived; wording differs). Thin sources may get an adjustment.
LimitsA DV01 limit and key-rate limits. Each needs an owner, a trip number and written steps. Risk leaders set them, not the trader.
Three jobsSuggest, approve, do. Three different people. The engine only suggests.
RecordsInputs, engine version, output, reader, approver, action. SEC Rule 17a-4 has tiered terms (about 3 and 6 years, first two easy to reach). Check live which rule binds the buyer.
Talking helperA person approves. Every number comes from the engine. It shows its reasons. It is on the model list and sampled.
GotchasCalling the engine “not a model” because it is just math. Calling a gap an error when the date basis or curve differed. Letting the proposer approve. A limit with no owner or no written step. Letting a language model state a number the engine did not give.

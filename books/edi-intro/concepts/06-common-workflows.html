Chapter SixOther Common EDI Workflows

A partner's catalog may list sixty transaction sets. Your first implementation needs about five. Knowing which five is the skill.

Order-to-cash is the spine, but real integrations grow limbs. Here is the surrounding territory, grouped by the industry that drives it — not so you can implement all of it, but so you can recognise what a partner is asking for and estimate it honestly.

Warehousing and freight

If a third-party logistics provider is in the picture, the warehouse pair appears: a 940 warehouse shipping order instructs the 3PL to ship, and a 945 warehouse shipping advice reports what actually left the building. The 945 is usually what your 856 is built from — another instance of chapter 4's rule that each document is derived from the previous one, not from the original order.

On the carrier side, load tenders, shipment status messages and freight invoices form their own small flow, generally handled by a TMS rather than by the order system.

Healthcare

Healthcare EDI in the US is a different world: the transactions are HIPAA-mandated, and the current production version is 5010, required since January 2012.

A different acknowledgment chain

Healthcare does not simply swap 997 for 999. A claim submission is typically answered by a TA1 (envelope), then a 999 (syntax and implementation guide), then a 277CA claim acknowledgment carrying the business-level accept or reject. Three responses, arriving at three different times, all of which your correlation model has to accommodate.

Choosing the first set

Here is the practical advice, and it is worth defending in a planning meeting. For a first implementation, support:

  • Inbound: one document type — the 850.
  • Outbound: the 997 (always, immediately), then the 855, 856 and 810.

That is a complete, useful, revenue-carrying integration. Everything else — 846, 852, 860, the warehouse pair — can be added later against the same pipeline, because by then the envelope handling, the partner profile, the canonical model and the acknowledgment machinery are all built and proven.

So what did we actually gain?

A defensible scope, and the vocabulary to read a partner's transaction list without alarm. Part III now builds the machine: chapter 7 lays out the pipeline stage by stage, and the next three chapters implement its middle.

Chapter FourThe Order-to-Cash Flow

Five documents move a purchase order from placed to paid. Learn how they join to each other and you have learned the shape of most EDI work.

Order-to-cash is the canonical EDI flow, and it is where almost every retail integration starts. Five transaction sets, each with an EDIFACT twin, each referring back to the last by a business identifier rather than by anything the transport provides.

Reading an 850

Inside the ST/SE envelope, an 850 is a header, some party loops, and a list of lines. Our running example, with the envelope stripped off:

ST*850*0001~
BEG*00*SA*PO-88213**20260815~   original / stand-alone order
REF*DP*STORE-42~              department
N1*ST*ACME RETAIL DC 7*92*DC07~ ship-to, buyer-assigned id
N3*1400 DOCK ROAD~
N4*COLUMBUS*OH*43230*US~
PO1*1*24*CA*18.50**BP*WID-1001*UP*000123456789~
PID*F****Widget, 10mm, boxed~
PO1*2*6*CA*42.00**BP*WID-2010~
CTT*2~                       line count — a free integrity check
SE*11*0001~

Three things are worth naming. BEG03 is the purchase order number — the identifier every later document will quote. The N1 loop uses a qualifier pair: ST says "this party is the ship-to", 92 says "the identifier that follows is buyer-assigned". And PO1 carries product identifiers the same way: BP for the buyer's part number, UP for a UPC.

Qualifiers everywhere

The qualifier-then-value idiom is EDI's answer to polymorphism: rather than defining a field per identifier type, one field carries the value and its neighbour says what kind of thing it is. It is compact, and it is the main friction when you map into a typed internal model (chapter 9).

How the documents join

There is no foreign key and no correlation header. Documents are joined by business identifiers copied from one into the next, and knowing which field carries the join is most of the integration work.

The 855 is the real answer

The purchase order acknowledgment tells the buyer whether the seller accepted the order, rejected it, or accepted it with changes — a substituted product, a reduced quantity, a different date. That third case is the one that matters architecturally: it means your canonical order model must be able to represent a difference between what was ordered and what was agreed, because the invoice will be built from the second one.

Invoice what shipped, not what was ordered

If the 855 reduced a line from 24 cases to 18, and the 856 says 18 cases went on the truck, then an 810 for 24 will be charged back. The invoice is built from the shipment, and the shipment is built from the acknowledgment. Follow that chain and the reconciliation disputes mostly disappear.

So what did we actually gain?

A concrete flow to build against, and one durable rule: every document in the chain is derived from the one before it, not from the original order. Chapter 5 takes the two acknowledgments seriously — what they prove, what they do not, and how to retry without ordering the same widgets twice.

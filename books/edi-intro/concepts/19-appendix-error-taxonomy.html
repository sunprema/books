Appendix CEDI Error Taxonomy

Eight failure classes, sorted by the only two questions that matter operationally: who can fix it, and does retrying help?

The point of a taxonomy is not tidiness. It is that retryable and non-retryable failures need opposite responses, and a system that maps everything to a single "error" status has thrown away the distinction it most needs.

The eight classes

What fails, who owns it, what to do
#ClassTypical causeResponse
1Transport Connection refused, timeout, partner SFTP full, VAN outage Retry with backoff. Alert only if it persists past the cadence window.
2Security / certificate Expired AS2 certificate, rotated SSH key, PGP key mismatch Retry is futile but harmless; the real fix is human. Alert immediately — no documents are arriving.
3Envelope Malformed ISA, control numbers unpaired, group count wrong Respond TA1. Do not retry. Partner must resend a corrected interchange.
4Syntax Missing required segment, bad element length or type, loop not closed Respond 997/999 with AK3/AK4 detail. Do not retry.
5Implementation guide Valid per the standard, disallowed by this partner's guide (unlisted UOM, missing REF*DP) Reject with the rule name. Usually resolved by a conversation, sometimes by a profile change.
6Mapping Unknown qualifier, unmapped code, ship-to that resolves to nothing Dead-letter. This is your bug or your reference data — the document is fine.
7Downstream system ERP unavailable, publish timeout, queue full Retry. The document is valid; only delivery failed. Idempotency makes this safe.
8Business rule Item discontinued, credit hold, quantity below minimum, price mismatch Not an error at all — it is a business answer. Respond in the 855, not the 997.

Which acknowledgment carries which class

The taxonomy maps neatly onto chapter 5's acknowledgment layers, which is a good sign that both are carved at the right joints:

ClassReported by
1, 2 — transport, securityNothing. No document exists. Only monitoring sees these.
3 — envelopeTA1
4, 5 — syntax, guide997 (retail) or 999 (healthcare); CONTRL in EDIFACT
6 — mappingInternal only. The partner sent a valid document.
7 — downstreamInternal only, unless it delays a business response past the agreed window.
8 — business rule855 / 865 / the relevant business response
Classes 1 and 2 are invisible by design

Note the first row: transport and certificate failures produce no document and therefore no error record. This is precisely why chapter 13 insists on alerting for silence and volume anomalies. An error dashboard will be perfectly green while a partner has been unable to reach you for three days.

Class 8 is not a failure

"We reject this order because the item is discontinued" is a normal commercial outcome, and it belongs in the 855 where the partner's order system will read it. Reporting it as a 997 rejection tells the partner their document was broken, which sends them looking for a bug that does not exist.

Store the class

Put the class on the record, not just the message. It is what drives the retry decision, the alert routing, and the dashboard breakdown — and it is what lets someone ask "how much of last month's failure volume was actually our mapping data?" and get an answer in one query.

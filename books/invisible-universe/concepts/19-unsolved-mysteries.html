Unsolved Mysteries

Nova had one more honest thing to show Mira before the journey's end: a map of everything the Standard Model — for all its precision — still cannot explain. A good detective story doesn't tie up every thread. This one, genuinely, doesn't either.

Mystery 1 — Matter vs. antimatter The Big Bang should have made equal amounts of matter and antimatter, which should have annihilated each other into pure energy, leaving no matter at all. Instead, matter won — narrowly — and the universe (and you) exist. Why matter won is still unanswered.
Mystery 2 — Dark matter's identity About 27% of the universe is dark matter, known only by its gravity. No dark matter particle has ever been directly detected, despite years of dedicated searches at CERN and elsewhere.
Mystery 3 — Why neutrinos have mass, and how much Neutrino oscillation proved neutrinos have mass, but nobody has measured their individual masses precisely, and nobody knows why they're so much lighter than every other massive particle in this book.
Mystery 4 — Gravity doesn't fit the model Electromagnetism, the weak force, and the strong force all fit inside the Standard Model's quantum framework. Gravity, so far, refuses to. No confirmed "graviton" or quantum theory of gravity exists yet.

Even the muon, which you met chapters ago, still has an open question attached to it: Fermilab's most precise measurement yet of its magnetic behavior sits in tension with some theoretical predictions — though whether that's a crack in the Standard Model or a disagreement between calculation methods is still being actively debated by physicists right now, as this book is being written.

Why this matters A field with open questions isn't a field that failed — it's a field that's still alive. Every particle in this book, from the electron to the Higgs boson, was once exactly this kind of open question, sitting unanswered until someone built the right experiment to answer it.
Quick check Name one thing the Standard Model has never explained. (Any of: the matter-antimatter imbalance, what dark matter is made of, the exact values of neutrino masses, or how gravity fits into the same quantum framework as the other three forces.)

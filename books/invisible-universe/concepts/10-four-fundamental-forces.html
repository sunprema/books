The Four Fundamental Forces

Mira had been meeting particles for chapters now — but particles alone don't explain why anything holds together, moves, or falls. For that you need forces. As far as physics has confirmed, there are exactly four: gravity, electromagnetism, the weak nuclear force, and the strong nuclear force — the one you already met, holding quarks together inside protons.

Strength isn't the only thing that separates them — range matters just as much. Gravity and electromagnetism both reach out to infinity, fading but never fully vanishing (that's why you can feel the Sun's gravity from 150 million kilometers away). The strong and weak forces are both confined to distances around the size of a proton or smaller — step outside that range and they simply switch off.

Why this matters Gravity is so much weaker than the other three that it's essentially irrelevant at the scale of individual particles — a single proton's gravitational pull on another proton is dwarfed by its electric repulsion by roughly 36 orders of magnitude. Gravity only becomes noticeable once you pile up an astronomical number of particles, like an entire planet's worth.

Physicists have spent a century looking for a single, unified explanation that produces all four forces from one underlying idea — the electroweak theory already unified electromagnetism and the weak force into one description at high enough energies. Gravity, so far, refuses to join the party: nobody has a confirmed quantum theory of gravity that fits inside the Standard Model. That gap is one of the genuine unsolved mysteries you'll meet again in Part VIII.

Notable women in particle physics Chien-Shiung Wu (1912–1997) experimentally proved the weak force violates parity symmetry in 1956–57 — a foundational result whose 1957 Nobel Prize went only to the theorists who predicted it. Fabiola Gianotti led the ATLAS collaboration during the 2012 Higgs boson discovery and became the first woman to serve as CERN's Director-General. Their work sits alongside thousands of other physicists, of every background, still actively expanding this field.

Glossary

Antimatter
Matter's mirror twin — same mass, opposite charge; annihilates on contact with matter.
Boson
A force-carrying particle (photon, gluon, W, Z, Higgs).
Color charge
The strong force's charge, labeled red/green/blue purely as a naming convention.
Confinement
Quarks are never found alone — pulling them apart creates new particles instead.
Dark matter
Invisible matter inferred only through gravity; ~27% of the universe; never directly detected.
Fermion
A matter particle (any quark or lepton), as opposed to a force-carrying boson.
Gluon
The strong force's massless carrier; uniquely, it interacts with other gluons.
Hadron
Any particle built from quarks (protons and neutrons are both hadrons).
Higgs field
A field filling space that gives elementary particles their mass through interaction.
Lepton
A matter particle that feels no strong force (electron, muon, tau, and their neutrinos).
Neutrino oscillation
A neutrino changing flavor mid-flight — proof neutrinos have mass.
Quark
A fractionally-charged matter particle that combines (never alone) to form protons, neutrons, and other hadrons.
Standard Model
Physics' current, well-tested description of all known elementary particles and three of the four forces.
Superposition
A quantum system holding more than one possible state at once, until measured.

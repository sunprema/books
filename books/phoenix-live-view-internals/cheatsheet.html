Cheatsheet

The callback signatures, the push_* family, and the diff rules from all ten chapters, condensed to what you'd actually want open in a second tab while writing a LiveView.

Callback signatures

mount/3

mount(params, session, socket)
Once per process birth (twice on first load: disconnected, then connected). Assign initial state. Gate side effects behind connected?(socket).

handle_params/3

handle_params(params, uri, socket)
After mount, and on every push_patch. Owns query params and URL-driven state.

render/1

render(assigns)
Pure function of assigns → HEEx. No queries, no side effects.

handle_event/3

handle_event(name, payload, socket)
A client interaction (phx-click, phx-submit, phx-change…). Payload is untrusted.

handle_info/2

handle_info(message, socket)
A message from another process — usually a PubSub broadcast.

handle_async/3

handle_async(name, {:ok, result} | {:exit, reason}, socket)
Completion of a start_async/3 task. Handle both branches.

What defeats change tracking

  • A function call with a side effect inside the template — {Accounts.count_online_users()} re-runs on every render.
  • A local <% x = … %> binding — the compiler can't see through it to the assigns it depends on.
  • Mutating socket.assigns via Map.put/3 instead of assign/3.

The one security rule

Never trust the client

Params, event payloads, and upload metadata are all client-supplied. Authorize in mount/3 (page access), handle_params/3 (navigation targets), and every handle_event/3 (the action itself) — against server-held data, never against anything read out of the payload. Broadcast on live_socket_id to force a reconnect (and a fresh authorization check) on logout.

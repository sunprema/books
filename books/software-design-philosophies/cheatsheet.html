C · Product invention checklist

Find (ch. 8)

  • ☐ Sum a cost paid per-instance that nobody has totaled (Kreps’ N² arithmetic)
  • ☐ Map the category on its two real axes; interrogate the evidenced empty cell (DuckDB)
  • ☐ Name the premise every product prices in; check it against current hardware/workloads (Tigani)
  • ☐ Re-derive one “weather” workflow from the actual job, as if the incumbent didn’t exist (Git)
  • ☐ Bar to clear: would the old workflow look absurd in retrospect?

Shape (chs. 2–3)

  • ☐ Write the new mental model in a sentence; list three operations it makes trivially cheap to reason about
  • ☐ Reach-around test: do the domain’s ten commonest operations stay inside the primitive?
  • ☐ Free-feature test: do three unplanned features fall out nearly free?
  • ☐ Complection test: is the primitive one idea, or two braided (payload+delivery, value+time)?
  • ☐ Count test: how many kinds? Could one fewer work?

Commit (chs. 4–7)

  • ☐ Write the refusals: which reasonable requests will this product say no to?
  • ☐ State each invariant in one sentence; station it at the layer that can actually keep it (end-to-end)
  • ☐ Draw the line: frozen core vs. churning edge; version the protocol from day one
  • ☐ Decide where history is primary, and keep that fold pure and deterministic
  • ☐ Budget SQLite-grade testing for the core — and only the core

Build & cross (ch. 9)

  • ☐ Build the smallest engine that makes the guarantee true end-to-end — no features on sand
  • ☐ Validate in the claimed scenario, against the alternatives a skeptic would name
  • ☐ Count steps from stranger’s curiosity to first success; delete most of them (pip install, one file, permissive license)
  • ☐ Meet users inside a familiar surface (SQL, an existing wire protocol) over the new model
  • ☐ Wedge workload: launch where the old model is most absurd, not for everyone
  • ☐ Commercial geometry: ubiquity for the core, revenue at the operated/collaborative edge
The 30-second summary

Great products reframe the problem (model), compress it into one deep un-braided unit (primitive), take explicit positions on omission, locality, time, and promises (philosophies), make one guarantee true in the smallest engine, and remove every step between curiosity and first success. Spend “right” on the invariants, “worse” everywhere else — and know which is which. The test of it all: what does your design refuse to do?

Resume reading

Most Kafka material stops at "produce a message, consume a message." That is the easy half. The hard half starts the day a second team builds on your topic, and every field you named casually becomes an interface you cannot change. This book is about that half.

How to read it

The order matters. Part I teaches you to think in contracts before you know the mechanics, because engineers who learn the mechanics first tend to design events that leak them. Part II then explains just enough of Kafka's internals to make reliability and scaling decisions honestly — not as trivia, but because ordering scope, durability and replay are contract properties too.

Part III is the centre of gravity: Avro evolution and Schema Registry governance. Parts IV and V are what you do with all of it. Part VI puts the whole thing together, including one chapter where it goes wrong on a Tuesday afternoon.

Every chapter closes by connecting a mechanism to a design decision. If a section ever reads like a configuration dump, we have failed and you should skip it.
